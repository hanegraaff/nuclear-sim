# Visual reference — Plan 01

The read-only prototype screenshots show Esri satellite imagery filling the viewport. `Missile.jpeg` has the left control stack hidden, making the imagery and bottom-right navigation controls useful references for this first map-only increment. Plan 01 intentionally does not include the missile, crosshair, effect fields, cloud profile, or controls; those are assigned to later roadmap plans.

The browser smoke test compares an initial **2208 × 1276** screenshot with a reviewed baseline using a synthetic tile fixture. That fixture proves map layout, viewport coverage, controls, and attribution without depending on live Esri tiles; it is not a visual target for the real satellite imagery. The deployed Site uses the live configured imagery source. Most full-page prototype references have the same viewport, so later visual comparisons can use this size with matching camera state.

No approved visual deviation from the Plan 01 map behavior is recorded.

## Plan 05 impact and single field

The fixed-tile **1508 × 1080** browser captures use a 21 kt Fat Man, one completed zoom-control click from the initial New York camera, and a ground target at (1000, 580) CSS pixels. [Impact at 50 ms](../tests/e2e/blast.spec.ts-snapshots/plan-05-impact-50ms-darwin.png), [expansion at 2300 ms](../tests/e2e/blast.spec.ts-snapshots/plan-05-expansion-2300ms-darwin.png), and [settled field](../tests/e2e/blast.spec.ts-snapshots/plan-05-settled-darwin.png) were inspected against `Nuke Blast with Fireball.png` and `Nuke Blast Top.png`. The impact has a warm white/yellow/orange bloom over the target, the expansion has a white/red shock ring, and the final field reads from light center through orange, magenta, and translucent purple. Terrain remains visible through the outer field. The source references use live Esri imagery and a different camera zoom; the synthetic-tile frames establish shape, color, timing, and layer order without depending on current imagery.

After review, the field mask samples geographic distance at each visible pixel rather than extending a local pixel scale across the full radius. The fireball, severe, and moderate palette anchors remain exact even when they fall between regular image samples. The settled center has a bright white-gold corona and white-hot core; the red launch reticle disappears at impact so it does not hide the light. The four crossing glare lines were removed after user testing. These optical accents preserve the requested “1000 suns” brightness, while the modeled kilometer radii and 4.6-second timing remain unchanged. The linked fixed-time baselines were re-inspected against the prototype images after this revision.

Remaining staged differences after Plan 06: pitched terrain refinements arrive in Plan 07; the raised 3D fireball dome arrives in Plan 08; and the cloud monitor arrives in Plan 09. The center is still a flat Canvas glow. Reset is a working single button in the existing compact panel; the prototype pairs it with the later Multi-Launch toggle. The prototype's short lower-edge scientific qualification is present.

## Plan 08 raised fireball

For V-02/V-03, the reviewed [pitched expansion](../tests/e2e/fireball.spec.ts-snapshots/plan-08-expansion-darwin.png) and [pitched settled](../tests/e2e/fireball.spec.ts-snapshots/plan-08-settled-darwin.png) use a 1508 × 1080 fixed-tile viewport, 21 kt Fat Man, two zoom-control clicks, a real 60° right-drag pitch, and a fixed ground target. The reference `Nuke Blast with Fireball.png` shows a raised center in a **single** launch, while `prototype-code/app/page.tsx` passes 3D fireballs to its custom layer only in mass mode. We favor the visible screenshot and Plan 08 requirement: the new single-launch view renders the dome. The reference also uses live Esri imagery, a 2208 × 1276 composition, and a different camera scale; the physical kilometer-scaled dome is smaller in this engineering view. The cloud-profile panel remains scheduled for Plan 09. The shader's procedural white, gold, and orange folds follow `prototype-code/app/fireball-renderer.mjs`; the reference's green GPU speckles are treated as rendering artifacts rather than an intended palette color.
