Compare the same device, camera, viewport, and animation phase before judging a difference. Automated screenshots guard against regressions in the approved implementation. The read-only prototype images guide manual review.

## Appearance and known limits

The final visual review exercised incoming flight, impact, expansion, settlement, the cloud instrument, all effect views, and multi-launch at 2208 × 1276, as well as phone controls, in background headless Chrome. The projectile, reticle, palettes, and labels retain the references' visual intent. Camera framing, live imagery, and scale vary from the supplied captures; the cloud is softer and less dense, and the Visible field omits the prototype's faint interior contours. No phone screenshot was supplied. The root README's [showcase](../../README.md#see-it-in-action) uses separate 1600 × 900 live-imagery captures with attribution, rather than fixed-tile regression baselines. Reduced motion deliberately shortens the launch and suppresses smoke, flashes, pulsing, and shock rings; the settled modeled field is unchanged.

The settled Blast follows the prototype's white-gold center, orange/red inner field, magenta middle, and translucent purple outside. The dashed ruler and colored markers stay on geographic destinations as the camera moves. A pitched view samples the visible ground through the current camera; its ring and glow deform with perspective rather than sliding in screen pixels. The raised WebGL dome appears in single and multi mode. This favors the visible `Nuke Blast with Fireball.png` over prototype code that restricts the custom fireball layer to mass mode.

A direct five-strike comparison used live Esri imagery at 1299 × 751 CSS pixels, DPR 1.7, approximately zoom 12.6 and pitch 60°. Its 2.00 km map bar matched the source label. A display-only intensity transfer localized the hot center; a bounded hue accent moved partial overlap from purple toward magenta/red while keeping five fireballs distinct. A fixed-tile sample changed from RGBA [88, 21, 117, 98] to [204, 39, 90, 124] with a second field: the red-minus-blue difference rose by 143, versus an alpha rise of 26. This is a visible gradient shift, not only opacity. A dense 17-impact live scene and an isolated older W54 field stayed visible after camera movement, though the fixed-size dense mask is softer and may fill over subsequent frames.

In the five-strike image, prototype fireballs measure about **30–40 output pixels** across; the near-matched candidate measured roughly **60–70 pixels**. The dome uses the prototype's kilometer-scaled geometry, so the difference is recorded without silently changing modeled radii. The cloud silhouette also remains somewhat less dense and soft than the prototype's photographic smoke. The fixed tile differs from Esri terrain by design, and live imagery coverage or horizon can vary with camera and tile availability. No dedicated prototype image establishes MIRV separation appearance, so its visible path is judged against prototype code and the missile/blast references rather than a claimed pixel match.

Fireball alignment after a page returns was checked with synthetic `visibilitychange` and `focus` events and an actual browser viewport resize. The controller discards a stale matrix and paints from a fresh MapLibre frame. Chromium did not emit native background or operating-system focus events in this test environment, so this evidence does not claim a real application-switch test. The sampled MIRV forward-path check likewise covers the launch camera states tested here, not every possible nonlinear perspective after later camera movement.
