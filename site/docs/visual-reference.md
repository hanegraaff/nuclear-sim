# Visual reference — Plan 01

The read-only prototype screenshots show Esri satellite imagery filling the viewport. `Missile.jpeg` has the left control stack hidden, making the imagery and bottom-right navigation controls useful references for this first map-only increment. Plan 01 intentionally does not include the missile, crosshair, effect fields, cloud profile, or controls; those are assigned to later roadmap plans.

The browser smoke test compares an initial **2208 × 1276** screenshot with a reviewed baseline using a synthetic tile fixture. That fixture proves map layout, viewport coverage, controls, and attribution without depending on live Esri tiles; it is not a visual target for the real satellite imagery. The deployed Site uses the live configured imagery source. Most full-page prototype references have the same viewport, so later visual comparisons can use this size with matching camera state.

No approved visual deviation from the Plan 01 map behavior is recorded.

## Plan 10 additive multi launch

For R-07/V-04/M-06, `Multi-Launch.png` shows five 21 kt Fat Man impacts at a pitched New York camera: individual small fireballs sit inside a shared yellow/orange center, surrounded by translucent red, pink, and purple fields. The left control reads “Multi-Launch Mode ON,” and a lower-left **2.00 km** map bar and four-band combined-damage legend replace the single ruler and geographic markers. The candidate follows that layout and retains separate impact domes. Direct Chromium interaction confirmed that a second missile can fly while the first field expands, legend markers open popups on hover and focus, turning the mode off keeps and displays the newest whole click group with its latest delivery's single ruler, and Reset clears the accumulated scene while preserving mode and camera.

The visual comparison used live Esri imagery at **1299 × 751 CSS pixels** and device pixel ratio **1.7** (about **2208 × 1277 output pixels**), a 21 kt Fat Man, zoom **12.6**, pitch **60°**, and five approximately matched targets. The 2.00 km bar matched the source label. The first comparison showed a yellow-orange sheet across much of the viewport, so a rendering-only `intensity^1.85` tone transfer localized the hot colors. A later overlap hue accent made a partially shared purple region advance toward magenta/red, but its first version washed the five-strike center white. The final accent tapers to zero as the display intensity reaches **0.7**. In the approved recheck, yellow/orange is concentrated near five distinct fireballs and the red/pink/purple outskirts let imagery show through, closer to the reference. A dense **17/17-impact** live pitched scene rendered its full field after pan and resize; inspected frames at **100 ms** and **1300 ms** after that camera change had no missing chunks. A separate fixed-tile run kept the oldest isolated W54 effect visible after 16 further impacts and a pan: its center alpha was 245 initially, 215 after accumulation, and 211 at the reprojected position. The reference provides no dense-state image, and these checks do not establish a fixed completion time.

A later direct fixed-tile Chromium recheck at **1508 × 1080 CSS pixels** confirmed the shared entry direction and the revised overlap color. Three missiles aimed at different targets all approached from the upper-right with separate curved trails at **3200 ms**. In the final fixed-tile recheck, a partial-overlap canvas sample changed from one field's RGBA **[88, 21, 117, 98]** to **[204, 39, 90, 124]** with two fields: red minus blue rose by **143** channel values while alpha rose by **26**. The visible sample moved from purple toward magenta/red, so the change is more than increased opacity. Domain radii and yields are unchanged. A whole click group remains visible after switching back to single mode; the ruler and markers describe its latest delivery.

The near-matched captures still differ in fireball size: candidate domes measure roughly **60–70 output pixels** across versus roughly **30–40 pixels** in the prototype image. The dome radius and envelope use the prototype's rendering math; target placement and camera scale were approximated, so this image-size difference is recorded without changing physical radii. Imagery coverage and the upper horizon vary with the camera and live tiles, and the final recheck no longer showed the earlier broad dark sky band. The automated browser path uses a fixed tile at 1508 × 1080 CSS pixels for deterministic behavior checks and saves two/five-strike frames; it is not a pixel-equality comparison with the live-Esri screenshot. MIRV branching and the upper-right effect views remain later roadmap work.
