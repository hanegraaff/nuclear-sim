# Visual reference and review states

The nine images in `prototype-screenshots/` are the visual target for this simulator. Most full-page references are 2208 × 1276 pixels. Compare the same device, camera, viewport, and animation phase before judging a difference. Browser baselines use a deterministic synthetic raster tile, while the prototype and published Site use live Esri World Imagery; tile content and camera composition cannot be compared pixel for pixel across those sources. Automated screenshots guard against regressions in the approved implementation. The read-only prototype images guide manual review.

## Appearance and known limits

The settled Blast follows the prototype's white-gold center, orange/red inner field, magenta middle, and translucent purple outside. The dashed ruler and colored markers stay on geographic destinations as the camera moves. A pitched view samples the visible ground through the current camera; its ring and glow deform with perspective rather than sliding in screen pixels. The raised WebGL dome appears in single and multi mode. This favors the visible `Nuke Blast with Fireball.png` over prototype code that restricts the custom fireball layer to mass mode.

A direct five-strike comparison used live Esri imagery at 1299 × 751 CSS pixels, DPR 1.7, approximately zoom 12.6 and pitch 60°. Its 2.00 km map bar matched the source label. A display-only intensity transfer localized the hot center; a bounded hue accent moved partial overlap from purple toward magenta/red while keeping five fireballs distinct. A fixed-tile sample changed from RGBA [88, 21, 117, 98] to [204, 39, 90, 124] with a second field: the red-minus-blue difference rose by 143, versus an alpha rise of 26. This is a visible gradient shift, not only opacity. A dense 17-impact live scene and an isolated older W54 field stayed visible after camera movement, though the fixed-size dense mask is softer and may fill over subsequent frames.

In the five-strike image, prototype fireballs measure about **30–40 output pixels** across; the near-matched candidate measured roughly **60–70 pixels**. The dome uses the prototype's kilometer-scaled geometry, so the difference is recorded without silently changing modeled radii. The cloud silhouette also remains somewhat less dense and soft than the prototype's photographic smoke. The fixed tile differs from Esri terrain by design, and live imagery coverage or horizon can vary with camera and tile availability. No dedicated prototype image establishes MIRV separation appearance, so its visible path is judged against prototype code and the missile/blast references rather than a claimed pixel match.

Fireball alignment after a page returns was checked with synthetic `visibilitychange` and `focus` events and an actual browser viewport resize. The controller discards a stale matrix and paints from a fresh MapLibre frame. Chromium did not emit native background or operating-system focus events in this test environment, so this evidence does not claim a real application-switch test. The sampled MIRV forward-path check likewise covers the launch camera states tested here, not every possible nonlinear perspective after later camera movement.

The Fallout comparison uses the default Fat Man and a pitched view in both 2208 × 1276 CSS pixels at DPR 1 and 1299 × 751 CSS pixels at DPR 1.7. The latter approximates the reference's 2208 × 1276 output while keeping the control panel at its likely CSS scale; its saved baseline is CSS-sized, and its full device-pixel capture is approximately 2208 × 1277. The source image does not establish its original browser DPR, so this is a composition comparison rather than a recovered camera specification. Both candidate baselines use the fixed tile and were inspected against `Fallout.png` for a bright yellow-white local core, curved orange/pink/purple tail, irregular contours, H+24 42.1 km eastward ruler, and four colored markers. Camera-independent scalar textures and cached geographic contours keep the full field attached during movement; deterministic physical-coordinate noise prevents camera-dependent contour shimmer. The approximate-DPR baseline was inspected for the contour rasterization change caused by geographic interpolation: the plume extent, palette, optical core, ruler, markers, and controls remain consistent with the reference intent. The candidate plume remains slimmer under pitch than the reference's rounder, softer screen-space lobes; this is a recorded visual difference, not an exact match. Live terrain and the more photographic prototype cloud remain additional differences.

Blast, eligible Fallout, and Audible tabs are implemented. The selector stays at the upper right on wide screens and moves below the instrument stack on narrow screens; a 430 × 844 pointer check verifies that view switching remains reachable without launching another strike. Single-mode Fallout frames the first nuclear envelope; single-mode Audible frames the latest retained hearing radius. The saved Blast camera returns only when the Blast tab is explicitly selected. A new map click returns to Blast without restoring that old frame, keeping the new reticle visible. Coarse Fallout textures show all retained plumes together before refinement; pan, zoom, pitch, and resize reproject valid textures instead of rebuilding their scalar model samples. In-motion browser checks cover the field itself, and high-zoom checks keep viewport-enclosing faces visible. Multi-mode switches preserve the camera and show a fixed map scale and four-band legend instead of a geographic ruler. The printed Gy bands are prototype screening labels rather than calculated dose, and the visible qualification identifies the surface-burst H+24 envelope and climatological wind proxy.

The Audible comparison uses the default Fat Man with a single settled impact and two overlapping impacts at 2208 × 1276 CSS pixels, DPR 1. The field grades continuously from a pale mint center through saturated green to a dark green soft edge, and the multi legend shares that palette. Its single dashed ruler and four markers describe geographic spans; the two-impact multi view shows the map-distance bar and Faint, Loud, Painful, and Deafening legend. The reference `Additive Blasts.png` is a pitched ten-impact live-imagery scene. The deterministic baselines use a top-down camera, two impacts, and a synthetic tile whose repeated coastline and grid create visible vertical seams under the translucent field. Those differences prevent a pixel match or direct judgment of the reference's pitched field shape, but the green progression, absence of hard ring boundaries, legend placement, and qualification text are comparable. On first single-mode entry, a complete coarse preview of up to ten deliveries is built before the camera ease within an 8,000-evaluation scene budget. Its projected sampling rectangle encloses the hearing circles beyond the starting viewport so the moving green field is not clipped at a straight preview-mask edge. The preview remains available even if a full starting-viewport mask completes before the first move; the moving camera uses the wider preview until its own settled mask is ready. The inspected 350 ms browser frame and shape checks at 120, 350, and 550 ms cover that transition; the synthetic tile still has its own straight seams beneath the translucent field. A separate 20 Tt browser path checks that a capped global field remains visible across sampled ground points at those same camera-ease times; its bounded preview includes adjacent world copies. Invalid horizon or wrap triangles can be omitted from temporary reprojection. First-time scenes beyond the preview limit may still fill in across several frames. The single baseline was inspected after a device-specific burst-height correction changed the displayed Fat Man radius from about 45.0 to 45.4 km; its small camera framing change shifted fixture seams. Audible is selectable only after completion, so its field does not replace the incoming, impact, or expansion Blast frames.
