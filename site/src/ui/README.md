# User interface

React mounts the full-viewport map and displays selection, launch status, cloud height, and inspection controls. Components subscribe to `SimulatorStore` snapshots and dispatch semantic actions; they do not calculate physical radii, branch targets, overlap intensity, or animation time. Packaged Geist fonts, compact charcoal panels, coral controls, and dense mono labels reproduce the prototype's visual hierarchy.

`DevicePanel` contains keyboard-accessible Scenario and Device tabs, grouped selectors, custom-yield input, Reset, a pressed-state Multi-Launch Mode button, Night, and Hide. Selecting a scenario prepares a device and requests camera movement without launching. Invalid custom text stays available for correction but prevents a map launch. A persistent polite status announces inbound, expanding, and complete phases even while controls are hidden; a MIRV status includes impacted and total warheads. A pending MIRV camera ease keeps Reset enabled even before a launch group exists. A control click never reaches the map launch target. Night reports its pressed state; Hide makes both left panels inert and moves focus to the Show Controls button. Restoring the panels returns focus to Hide. The effect selector remains accessible while the panels are hidden.

`PreviewSelect` preserves native mobile selection and enhances eligible desktops wider than 600 CSS pixels with a fine pointer and hover. Its native control retains DOM focus while a lazy grouped list exposes the active descendant. Scrolling or navigating options shows `SelectionPreviewOverlay`; opening alone does not. The panel is informational and has no focusable controls. Dropdown blur, window blur, closure, Resize, Reset, Hide, and tab changes dismiss it immediately. Outside dismissal cannot become a map launch. Pointer/wheel browsing tracks the visible row after scrolling; keyboard navigation scrolls the active option into view.

The preview renders researched weapon facts and exterior outlines, a logarithmic Hiroshima energy gauge, or a scenario image with its dark event description. Estimates, reported claims, unknown values, and conceptual studies remain qualified. On constrained desktops the details region scrolls by wheel or **Shift+PageUp / Shift+PageDown** without transferring focus from the dropdown; a narrow desktop uses a shorter option list above a wider panel. Candidate changes reset the details position. `PreviewImageCache` fetches only the active local asset, decodes asynchronously, retains at most two images and 16 MiB of estimated decoded pixels, and releases object URLs on dismissal. Aborted or stale results cannot replace the current candidate or revive the panel; failed images leave facts or event text readable. Browser HTTP caching is separate from this application-owned bound. [Research](../../docs/catalog-research.md) and [artwork notes](../../docs/preview-artwork.md) document the content limits.

`MapViewport` exposes a focus-revealed **Launch at map center** button for keyboard users. It sends the map's current geographic center through the same launch preparation and routing as a pointer click. The app observes `prefers-reduced-motion` and passes that preference to the store for subsequent launches; reduced-motion rendering omits intense transient effects while preserving the final modeled state.

`MapViewport` mounts and disposes the rendering-owned controller, passes geographic launch frames, camera requests, map tone, and panel visibility across the boundary, and receives readiness, camera, and inspection geometry through callbacks. It asks the application to prepare and freeze a click's device and geographic targets before the map frames a MIRV. A pending target reticle paints immediately while an unsafe route is reframed; the selected device may change during that delay without changing the queued launch. Reset cancels pending clicks, and exhausted framing attempts show a recoverable error message. Map imagery errors show a separate retry action without blocking the rest of the UI. For a narrow MIRV group, the UI supplies the lower edge of the control stack in CSS pixels so the map controller can frame otherwise hidden impacts; rendering does not inspect a React class or component. The map and effect canvases remain transparent to pointer input except at intentional controls and marker targets.

In single mode, `BlastInspectionOverlay` positions transparent focusable hit targets at the renderer's four visible marker rings after expansion. Hover, focus, click-to-pin, and Escape update inspection state. One tooltip explains the current zone and its modeled span and area, clamped inside the viewport. In multi mode, `MultiLaunchLegend` provides four fixed gradient markers with the same interactions and a camera-derived distance bar; its quantitative span belongs to the latest individual strike, while the colors show combined field intensity. Switching back displays the newest whole group and its latest delivery's ruler and markers.

`EffectViewSelector` exposes Blast, Audible, Visible, and eligible Fallout tabs after the retained scene settles; Fallout eligibility comes from application state. The selector sits at the upper right on a wide viewport and below the instrument stack at the lower left on narrow screens so pointer controls remain reachable. Its clicks stop map launch input. `FalloutInspectionOverlay` uses renderer-provided marker positions in single mode and application-provided newest-nuclear-delivery zones in the multi legend. The four bands support hover, keyboard focus, pinning, and Escape, with viewport-bounded popups explaining the prototype Gy label, downwind span, and approximate area. React performs no fallout physics. `MapViewport` displays the surface-burst H+24 and climatological-wind qualification while Fallout is active.

`AudibleInspectionOverlay` uses the renderer's geographic marker positions in single mode and the latest delivery's modeled bands in the multi legend. Faint, Loud, Painful, and Deafening controls support the same hover, keyboard focus, click-to-pin, and Escape interaction. Their popups give an illustrative experience, peak-dB phrase, and kilometer span; the map footer qualifies the outdoor low-frequency estimate for weather and terrain. The green field itself remains a smooth wash without band outlines. `VisibleInspectionOverlay` follows the same single-marker and multi-legend interaction with Faint, Clear, Brilliant, and Blinding viewing descriptions, kilometer spans, and a Visible radius label. Its blue field has no hard band outlines; the footer states the 1.7 m clear-air direct-fireball assumption. A new launch returns to Blast at the clicked camera and clears stale inspection; only an explicit Blast-tab selection restores the saved single-view camera.

`CloudProfileMonitor` sits below the controls on screens wider than 600 CSS pixels. Text announces the current modeled height and phase; a decorative, assistive-technology-hidden Canvas paints the scale and silhouette. It follows the first ignited warhead in the latest MIRV group and labels height as per warhead. Nuclear and conventional devices receive different plume wording. Reset returns the monitor to standby without changing selection, mode, or camera. The monitor is hidden at narrower widths, where controls compact to leave map space and short screens further compact the multi-strike scales. Effect tabs, qualifications, legends, navigation and attribution occupy separate regions, and Blast and Fallout popups flip above the tabs when necessary.
