# Ground effect rendering

`BlastCanvasRenderer` paints one immutable strike above the satellite map. Its public `render` method consumes a launch, elapsed milliseconds, and CSS-pixel viewport dimensions; `invalidateProjection` drops the cached field after camera or viewport movement. `blastExpansion` and `blastColor` expose the prototype timing curve and kilometer-based presentation anchors for focused tests.

`buildBlastDistanceGrid` unprojects each visible mask sample through the camera, then measures great-circle distance from the stored geographic target. It returns kilometer distances without a Canvas dependency. The renderer caches this grid and its exact color samples between animation frames, then applies the animated radius and edge feather to an offscreen mask. Camera movement, pitch, rotation, resize, strike replacement, and Reset invalidate the cache. Invalid sky samples remain transparent.

At zero pitch, the white-gold center keeps its screen-space gradient. At nonzero pitch, `groundGlowColor` samples the same geographic kilometer grid as the field; the renderer caches its offscreen light mask alongside the field. This makes the optical core lie on the ground plane without changing physical damage radii. A separate WebGL dome rises above this 2D ground light.

This area depends on immutable application launch data, the pure domain model, and the map camera adapter. It never computes yields or owns the animation clock.

Settled inspection strokes paint in a second Canvas above the WebGL dome. The field and optical ground glow stay on the lower Canvas; both canvases use the same camera frame and viewport size. If the separate inspection context is unavailable, the renderer falls back to its single-canvas path.

`MultiBlastCanvasRenderer` receives immutable launch frames from the map controller and builds **one shared viewport ground grid** of geographic sample coordinates. Each impact's target and kilometer radii are compared with those coordinates in turn; per-sample peak and sum arrays feed the pure domain overlap function, and four base-color bytes retain the strongest field’s single-reference RGBA. These accumulators cost 12 bytes per cell, in addition to one shared geographic grid and the output bitmap. No per-strike distance grid is retained. The camera grid is reused while projection and sample tier stay fixed, then discarded on pan, zoom, pitch, bearing, resize, Reset, or disposal. A completed field bitmap is cached between unchanged redraws. A MIRV group contributes each warhead as its own field at its own arrival time, even if multi mode is off.

`blastPalette.ts` defines the single-reference distance/color/opacity anchors and expanding purple feather used by both Blast paths. The shared compositor stores the strongest field’s exact RGBA bytes alongside peak and sum intensity. An isolated sample copies those bytes unchanged, so switching launch mode cannot dim or recolor it. `multiBlastPalette.ts` applies a bounded warm accent only when `sum > peak`, advancing actual overlap through the lower-left legend. The accent is at most 0.16, scaled by up to 1.2 times the weaker overlap and tapered to zero in already hot cores. Opacity remains at least the strongest isolated sample’s alpha. A 4,096-entry overlap color table bounds quantization error to one byte. This changes presentation, never a strike’s yield, radius, timing, or pure combined intensity. Fallout retains a separate palette module and its existing rendering behavior.

The compositor uses a shared ground mask of **560 × 380** samples below six strikes, **420 × 280** for six to fifteen, and a fixed **320 × 220** from sixteen onward. Keeping that dense tier fixed prevents a small W54 field from disappearing when many later strikes accumulate. It also means total composition work can grow with strike count; the hard per-repaint limits are **600,000** geodesic evaluations and **1,024** strike candidates, with unfinished work continuing on later animation frames. A conservative distance bound skips only fields proven unable to touch the visible ground. After a camera change, a partial field can appear while the rest is composed; dense fields may look softer, but all visible strikes are processed when the work finishes. These are visual work and memory tradeoffs, not changes to modeled distances. Recent smoke sampling is separately bounded. Only the newest eight impacted deliveries receive per-frame decorative ground-glow and shock-ring work, and pitched glow bitmaps are cached within that admission limit. This cap does not remove older geographic fields, WebGL fireballs, or application strikes. Multi mode omits the single ruler and dividing rings; a retained multi-delivery group in single mode keeps its complete combined field and paints the latest delivery's inspection ruler above it.

## Fallout field

`FalloutCanvasRenderer` consumes settled launch frames and includes only deliveries with an immutable nuclear `FalloutFootprint`. The pure model evaluates deposition in great-circle east/north kilometers around each impact. Rendering samples that kernel into a strike-local scalar texture, so the same data remains valid when the camera moves. Local skirts, the curved downwind lobe, surface-wind shear, and fine contour variation all stay fixed in physical coordinates.

Every retained field receives a complete coarse texture before any field is refined. Preview samples share a scene allowance of roughly 12,000 evaluations, with at most 1,600 texels per field. Later animation frames refine one already-visible field at a time, using at most 24,000 samples per field and sharing a 600,000-texel scene allowance. A changed retained count redistributes that allowance; replacing a footprint discards its stale entry. Lower-resolution previews can look softer, but one plume does not wait for another to finish before appearing.

`falloutTexture.ts` keeps scalar samples and a small geographic mesh. `projectFalloutTexture.ts` projects that mesh through the current camera into a screen mask capped at 560 × 380 samples. Each field contributes its scalar intensity through additive Canvas composition; palette lookup happens after the sum is bounded at one. Colors therefore advance with overlap without summing device yields or claiming additive dose. Triangles outside the viewport, invalid horizon projections, and discontinuous world-wrap faces are skipped. Valid faces larger than the viewport remain clipped and visible at high zoom. Texture coordinates beyond the antipodal chart are transparent rather than folded into a second copy of an extreme field.

Pan, zoom, pitch, bearing, and resize rebuild the projected mask without resampling cached physics. `falloutContours.ts` extracts geographic contour segments from the texture once and reprojects them with the same camera as the mesh and inspection ruler. Marker focus can reuse the completed mask while repainting inspection artwork. View changes, Reset, and disposal cancel pending refinement and release stale textures. Single mode paints the first nuclear delivery's scalar contours, curved ruler, and markers; multi mode uses the UI legend and map scale.

A separate screen-blended white-yellow glow gives ground zero the prototype's optical brightness. Its center remains the geographic impact, and its drawing radius derives from the projected local kilometer radius with a viewport cap. It does not change the scalar field, contour thresholds, inspection spans, or areas. The normalized values and printed Gy labels are screening illustration, not measured deposition or calculated radiation dose; see [model assumptions](../../../docs/model.md).

## Audible and Visible range fields

`RangeCanvasRenderer` supplies the shared camera sampling and cache for `AudibleCanvasRenderer` and `VisibleCanvasRenderer`. It unprojects mask sample centers lazily, measures great-circle distance to each settled delivery, and asks the chosen pure model for smooth normalized intensity. It combines those values with the bounded, order-independent peak-and-sum contract before one palette lookup. Audible paints dark green to mint for outdoor hearing; Visible paints navy through sky blue to ice white for direct sight of the luminous fireball. Each model's four experience bands guide ruler markers and its legend without creating hard color rings. Both fields fade to zero 12% beyond the nominal labeled radius. dB phrases and viewing descriptions are illustrative copy, not measurements at a map point.

The raster is at most about 480 × 320 samples, then scales smoothly to the viewport. A repaint visits at most **24,000** field/sample cells and **256** field candidates synchronously; unfinished work continues on animation frames. Its sample-point cache and peak/sum arrays stay fixed to one candidate mask, so work can grow with strike count without a per-strike mask. For an unpitched, north-up Mercator camera with no possible repeated world copies, `rangeFieldBounds` projects a conservative 1.12-radius cap and skips a field only when the whole cap is offscreen. Pitched, rotated, near-polar, wrap-ambiguous, and low-zoom views that may show repeated worlds use the full mask; one projected cap cannot safely bound both world copies.

On first entry to either range view in single mode, `primeFirstEntry` completes a coarse field **before** the camera ease begins, for scenes of up to ten deliveries. Its shared budget is at most **8,000 field/sample evaluations** across the scene. The renderer projects each delivery's range circle and samples one rectangle enclosing the circles and their soft tails, even where that rectangle extends beyond the starting viewport. The rectangle limits work; the smooth geographic intensity determines visible opacity, so a straight rectangle edge is not an intended field boundary during the ease. If that projection is unavailable or too remote, a bounded overscanned fallback still supplies a complete coarse preview where it contains visible field pixels. At extreme yields whose soft tail reaches the antipode, a bounded rectangle covers the starting view and adjacent Mercator world copies so the global field remains visible during the ease. Scenes larger than ten deliveries use the ordinary chunked path.

The renderer retains a completed mask or first-entry preview and a small geographic reprojection mesh. During pan, zoom, pitch, bearing, or resize, `audibleReprojection.ts` projects that field into the current camera without reevaluating model distance. Flat, unpitched Mercator motion uses one affine transform. Pitched motion warps a 12 × 8 ground mesh into a reused scratch raster capped at 512 × 384 samples, restores palette opacity after triangle-clip antialiasing (while keeping very faint Visible rim pixels faint), then draws the result once to reduce dark seams between mesh cells. Invalid horizon or wrap-crossing triangles are omitted. Camera changes cancel stale candidate chunks but keep the visible field. If the full starting-viewport mask finishes before `easeTo` emits its first move, the wider overscanned preview is still retained for that transition; it is preferred once movement begins so the old viewport edge cannot cut across the range circle. After camera motion settles, a fresh mask is composed in bounded chunks while the old field remains reprojected; the completed candidate replaces it in one swap. A changed strike set, view switch, Reset, or disposal releases the old scene instead. This avoids a partial new field replacing a complete one during camera movement, though invalid reprojection triangles may be omitted and dense first-time scenes outside the preview path can still fill in over several frames. Very large or highly pitched scenes may look softer than the live prototype. A single delivery receives a geographic dashed ruler and markers on the separate inspection canvas. Multi mode keeps the combined field but uses the UI's fixed mode-specific legend and map-distance scale. `audiblePalette.ts` and `visiblePalette.ts` supply raster colors and matching legend gradients, so each key follows its rendered field.
