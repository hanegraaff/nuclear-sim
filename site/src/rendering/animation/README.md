# Animation rendering

`flightGeometry.ts` and `MissileCanvasRenderer.ts` draw the red target, amber-white carrier, thin smoke trail, and MIRV branches on a transparent Canvas. The map controller supplies immutable launch frames and current camera projection. The route's start, control, aim, and warhead targets remain geographic after a click. For every frame, the renderer projects them together and samples the screen-space curve, so camera movement does not detach the artwork.

A MIRV paints one shared carrier and reticle until separation at about 60% of normal flight. `screenMirvPoint` follows the carrier up to the split and uses a branch with matching position and screen-space tangent before reaching each target at its own arrival time. Carrier smoke ends at the split; each branch grows its own trail. Multi-launch groups enter from one general direction but keep separate geographic routes. Dense scenes reduce smoke sampling. The renderer receives elapsed milliseconds and never schedules frames or changes a device profile.

`blastTiming.ts` supplies the fast-first expansion curve shared by ground effects and raised fireballs. `fireballGeometry.ts` provides `fireballEnvelope`, `hemisphereVertices`, `localMercatorMatrix`, `groundMetersPerPixel`, and `fireballMeshDetail`. Their units are documented at each API: normalized progress, geographic degrees, kilometers, CSS pixels, and MapLibre zoom. Geometry helpers require no browser or GPU. The hemisphere uses the modeled fireball radius for both horizontal radius and height; double-precision translation is completed before upload as Float32.

`FireballRenderer` owns a small WebGL2 program, mesh buffers, and transparent canvas. MapLibre's custom layer supplies the camera matrix; pan, zoom, pitch, and world wrapping move every dome with its target. `renderMany` draws separate MIRV and multi-launch domes in one pass and conservatively skips only fireballs fully outside the viewport clip volume. Missing context, shader or buffer failure, and layer failure leave the Canvas flash and field available; partial GPU allocations are released. Replacement and Reset clear the image, and disposal releases resources and listeners. Texture, halo, smoke, and cooling curves are optical illustrations rather than temperature, radiation, or ballistic predictions.
