# Nuke Simulator Site source

This directory builds the user-facing static Site. The full-screen MapLibre satellite map carries the prototype-style control panel, independent cloud instrument, animated missiles, ground effects, and WebGL fireballs. The public product has one entry point; the source packages below keep selection, physical estimates, state transitions, and drawing separate.

## Browsing devices and scenarios

Eligible desktops enhance the native selector with a grouped option list and an informational preview. Opening or focusing the dropdown alone shows no panel; scrolling or navigating options previews the active candidate while the dropdown retains focus. Browsing never commits a device, moves the camera, or launches. Selection uses the normal click/Enter path. Dropdown blur, closure, window focus loss, Reset, Hide, tab changes, or leaving the eligible layout clears the preview and its outer scrolling viewport. Wheel input, scrollbar dragging, and **Shift+PageUp / Shift+PageDown** scroll the popup while keeping focus on the dropdown. A constrained desktop places the preview beneath a shorter list so both remain usable. Phones and devices without a fine pointer and hover retain native selection and request no preview code or artwork.

Each weapon dossier identifies the displayed weapon, projectile, warhead, or carrier and separates its exterior dimensions and mass from modeled yield. Facts qualify estimates with **(est)** and distinguish reported claims, unknown values, and inapplicable fields. The weapon popup ends at these facts; optional inapplicable payload/fill rows are omitted, while provenance remains in the [catalog research ledger](docs/catalog-research.md). Scenario panels show complete development-generated red photographic illustrations at their natural 3:2 ratio, followed by concise dark historical accounts or clearly conditional hypothetical descriptions of 30–45 words. The popup scrolls externally rather than cropping its image. See [artwork provenance and prompts](docs/preview-artwork.md) and [model limits](docs/model.md). Data and the preview UI load on demand, artwork follows only the active candidate, and the two-image/16 MiB application-owned cache is cleared on dismissal. No generation or research service runs in the browser.

## What happens after a click

The map converts a click into a geographic aim point; a keyboard user can instead focus **Launch at map center** to target the current center. The application freezes the selected device and a MIRV's deterministic geographic targets at that point, before any camera movement. The map chooses a viewport-edge carrier route; the application store keeps the resulting launch group and advances it with an injected monotonic clock. A normal click carries one impact. A MIRV click uses its catalog count and **per-warhead** yield for three or ten distinct impacts. One carrier normally branches near 60% of flight. Geographic route planning moves separation earlier or lengthens the incoming approach if needed to keep the warheads moving forward; the map also checks the projected route for the current pitch and bearing before launch. The targets and their physical radii do not move. The same group survives a multi-to-single mode switch.

The catalog contains 52 fixed choices. Its seven conventional weapons are sorted by ascending TNT-equivalent yield, including five smaller weapons with approximate pressure-based estimates. The values represent specified explosive fills and are screening estimates, not measured forecasts; [model notes](docs/model.md) document their sources and limits. When the device requests reduced motion, the launch reaches the same modeled effect through a shorter flight and expansion, with smoke, flashes, shock rings, and other intense motion suppressed. A persistent polite status announces launch phases even if controls are hidden.

The renderer projects geographic routes through the current camera for every frame, keeping the missile, reticles, fireballs, fields, and markers attached during pan, zoom, pitch, and resize. If an entry or spread is unsafe at the current high zoom or narrow view, a red reticle appears immediately and the map eases for roughly 450 ms before dispatching the frozen click. Multi-mode clicks wait in order; Reset and replacement cancel pending work. A failed route after bounded framing attempts is reported without disabling the map. A narrow spread that would hide almost all settled centers can receive an additional one-time fit below the controls. When a hidden page returns or the window regains focus, the fireball layer discards its stale camera matrix and requests a fresh projection. In single mode, a settled field has four geographic zone boundaries, a dashed ruler, and inspectable markers. In multi mode, click groups accumulate, carriers share a general entry direction, and bounded field intensities move overlapping Blast samples toward warmer colors and overlapping range samples toward brighter green or blue. The lower-left distance bar and four-band legend replace the single ruler. Reset cancels animation and clears every group while retaining device, mode, and camera.

## Settled effect views

Once every retained delivery has completed and no launch is pending, Blast, eligible Fallout, Audible, and Visible views can be selected. Availability follows the retained impacts rather than the currently selected device: conventional-only scenes have no Fallout tab, mixed scenes include only nuclear fields, and a MIRV contributes a separate footprint for each warhead using its individual yield. Audible and Visible are available for conventional and nuclear impacts. A new launch returns to Blast while preserving the clicked camera and visible target; Reset removes every view's effects and inspection state.

Fallout shows an H+24 surface-burst screening envelope driven by a deterministic latitude-band wind proxy. Its warm local core, curved irregular plume, purple/pink/orange bands, and four inspectable markers reproduce the prototype. In single mode, the map frames the first nuclear footprint and restores the saved Blast camera when the Blast tab is selected; a retained MIRV still renders all its warhead fields. Multi mode preserves the camera and replaces the geographic ruler with a map scale and four-band legend describing the newest nuclear delivery. Overlap sums normalized field intensity, bounded at one, without inventing a combined device or dose. The printed Gy bands and health descriptions are illustrative prototype copy, not computed exposure or live meteorology.

Audible uses a separate bounded radius for each impact and a continuous dark-green-to-mint field with a soft edge. Its four outer-to-inner hearing experiences are Faint, Loud, Painful, and Deafening. In single mode, a dashed geographic ruler and marker popups show the modeled kilometer spans; selecting Audible frames the latest retained delivery, and an explicit Blast selection restores the saved camera. In multi mode, the camera stays put while a fixed gradient legend and distance bar explain the newest delivery's ranges. The same bounded, order-independent field composition intensifies overlap without turning several warheads into one virtual device. The dB phrases are illustrative copy, not calculated sound pressure at a location; actual audibility depends strongly on weather and terrain. Visible uses the same range presentation for a distinct direct-fireball horizon: an ice-white center fades through sky blue to navy. Its Faint, Clear, Brilliant, and Blinding bands appear on the single geographic ruler or the multi legend. A single Visible selection frames the latest delivery; an explicit Blast selection restores the saved camera. The 1.7 m observer and clear-air qualification describe a geometric sightline to the luminous fireball, not visibility of a later cloud or a measured brightness reading.

On screens wider than 600 CSS pixels, the cloud monitor follows the latest group's first ignited warhead through growth and modeled peak. Nuclear and conventional silhouettes differ; MIRV wording identifies the per-warhead scale. The monitor is hidden at narrower widths to make room for the map. All displayed distances and heights are illustrative estimates. See [model notes](docs/model.md) and [visual reference](docs/visual-reference.md).

The header's Night button changes the satellite raster's saturation, contrast, and brightness and adds a dark vignette. It leaves the modeled effect fields, marker locations, camera, and Esri attribution intact. Hide slides the left controls and cloud monitor offscreen and exposes a keyboard-focusable Show Controls button. The effect selector stays on the map. Hiding removes the desktop left or narrow-screen top camera padding, then the controller repaints fields and markers through the updated projection; restoring the panels reverses that padding. Launch and inspection state, device choice, effect view, and map tone persist across the toggle. Reset clears strikes but retains the map tone and panel visibility.

On narrow screens the control stack becomes compact and the cloud monitor is hidden, leaving an open region of the map for launches. Effect tabs, model qualifications, multi-strike scales, navigation controls, and expanded attribution have separate space near the bottom. Marker popups move above the tabs when needed. Short screens further compact the multi-strike scales.

## Source boundaries

| Area | Responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| [`src/catalog/`](src/catalog/README.md) | Exact device and scenario choices, labels, yields, and coordinates. |
| [`src/domain/`](src/domain/README.md) | Pure geodesy, blast, audible and visible ranges, MIRV spread, cloud, deterministic wind/fallout, zone-area, and normalized overlap calculations. |
| [`src/application/`](src/application/README.md) | Selection, launch groups, injected clock, phases, effect-view eligibility, inspection state, and model-derived presentation. |
| [`src/rendering/`](src/rendering/README.md) | MapLibre camera and imagery, Canvas fields and missiles, WebGL fireballs, and geographic overlays. |
| [`src/ui/`](src/ui/README.md) | React controls, map mount, status, instrument, and accessible inspection. |
| [`tests/`](tests/README.md) | Deterministic unit, integration, and browser verification. |

Model output never contains screen pixels or drawing objects. Application state never owns MapLibre or React components. Rendering receives immutable launch frames and uses one map camera to project them. Blast uses a shared multi-field grid with bounded storage and chunked work. Fallout instead caches scalar textures in each impact's physical coordinates: every retained field gets a complete coarse preview before finer detail is sampled. A geographic mesh and cached contour segments follow pan, zoom, pitch, and resize without resampling those textures. Audible and Visible share one range-renderer design, with separate radius models, palettes, and inspection copy. Each uses a fixed-resolution mask with bounded work chunks. On first single-mode entry, up to ten deliveries receive a complete coarse field before the camera eases, within a shared 8,000-evaluation budget. Its geographic sampling extends beyond the starting viewport to cover the range circles as they move into view; the sampling rectangle is not meant to appear as a hard field edge. The preview remains available for that first move even if the starting-viewport mask finishes first. An extreme field reaching the antipode uses a bounded preview across adjacent world copies. The renderer conservatively skips distant fields only in straightforward overhead views; pitched, rotated, polar, and wrap-ambiguous views sample the whole mask. A completed range mask or first-entry preview stays geographically aligned through camera movement by reprojection, without repeating the field's geodesic calculations on every camera frame. When movement ends, a bounded rebuild uses the settled camera and replaces the old field only after completion. Each view combines overlapping intensities before its palette lookup. Shared texture and screen-mask budgets trade spatial detail for bounded storage. The map scale estimates distance at the camera center, especially approximate on a pitched view.

## Engineering setup and delivery

Use Node.js 22.13 or newer. In this directory, run `npm ci` and, when needed, `npx playwright install chromium`. `npm run verify` runs type checking, lint, unit and integration tests, a build, and Playwright browser checks. `npm run dev` and `npm run preview` support engineering inspection. Browser tests replace live Esri raster tiles with a deterministic fixture; production uses Esri World Imagery with visible attribution and a retry notice if imagery fails.

The tracked `.openai/hosting.json` points the existing Site to the static `dist/` build. The root GitHub repository is authoritative; [publishing instructions](../docs/sites-publishing.md) copy an exact pushed source tree into a clean temporary release checkout, verify it, and publish to the same Site. Prototype code and screenshots are read-only references outside this directory.
